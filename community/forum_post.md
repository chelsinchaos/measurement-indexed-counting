**Subject:** Independent executable check of family 378 (The Exponential Time Hypothesis is False), plus a structural note on its Section 2.7 construction

I built an exact, executable model of the counting pipeline of the ETH manuscript (family 378) at toy scale and ran it against brute force. It is a different kind of check from the Lean development: it certifies much less, but every object in it can be printed and inspected.

What it implements (Python, standard library, exact rational arithmetic): the clause polynomials and replacement forms, measurement designs in the sense of Definition 2.1 with condition (4) verified at every point rather than assumed, the measurement-tuple identity (2.6), the restricted sums and inclusion–exclusion identity (2.9) over truncated power series, Hermite interpolation of H from Taylor data, and the paper's own Section 2.7 construction (auxiliary coordinates, swap matrices, interpolated f, eigenvector measurement rows, schedules, copies) at r = 2, Q ∈ {2, 3}, R ∈ {2, 4} and r = 3.

What it finds: identity (2.6) holds at every design point on every run (including rational values such as H(1) = −12561/32 for the Section 2.7 design at n = 12), and H(0) = #F is recovered exactly through the full pipeline at n = 10, 12, 14 (e.g. n = 14: 20 points, 580 restricted sums, 40 Hermite conditions, H(0) = 3,108). A second, separately written script expands H(T) symbolically and confirms every recorded value and Taylor coefficient.

One structural remark that I did not see in the manuscript: under the Step 4 schedules, the within-group clause hypergraph has exploration offspring mean 4/r², so it is critical at r = 2 (largest component ≈ n^{2/3}, measured) and subcritical for r ≥ 3 (largest component ≤ 15 of 1,333 variables at n = 4,000). The group sums therefore factorize over bounded components in the paper's regime. This changes nothing about the theorem — under (2.19) the group-enumeration term is about 1/256 of the Taylor-data term in the exponent — but it is a fact about the construction worth recording.

Code, dataset, logs and a full report are in https://github.com/chelsinchaos/measurement-indexed-counting; a NOTES.md with the same content is proposed to the community fork (mathcommunity/oai-math-revised).
